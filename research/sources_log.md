## 3. Answers to PRD §7 Research Questions (T3.3)

> **Explicit Scope Note (PRD §6.3):** Company A is an anonymous wireless operator from the GCI course dataset (`100,000` customers, originating from the Teradata/Duke CRM churn tournament `[7]`) and is **not claimed to be an Iraqi operator**. We use Iraq's mobile market (`[1]`–`[6]`) as a concrete local deployment lens as recommended by `tutorial.ipynb` §1.2.

1. **Q1 — Market state (subscribers, penetration, operators, shares):**
   Iraq's mobile market reached **`48.1 million` cellular connections** in early 2025 (**`103%` of the `46.5 million` population**, up `+5.2%` YoY from `46.0 million` / `100.0%` in early 2024), with `82.9%` of connections on 3G/4G mobile broadband `[1, 2]`. Under the regulatory oversight of the **Communications and Media Commission (CMC)** `[3]`, the market is led by **Zain Iraq (`19.7 million` active subscribers in 2024, up from `17.9M` in 2023)** `[4]` and **Asiacell (`19.13 million` active subscribers in 2024, up from `17.69M` in 2023)** `[5]`, which together serve over **`80%`** of active lines alongside regional third operator Korek Telecom `[3, 6]`. Because connection penetration already exceeds `100%` of the population, operator revenue growth depends on **retaining active high-value subscribers** rather than greenfield first-time adoption `[1, 6]`.

2. **Q2 — Local churn drivers (competition, multi-SIM, price/regulation, network quality, 4G/5G/eSIM):**
   In Iraq and across the MENA region, mobile connections exceed population (`103%`) because consumers routinely hold **multiple SIM cards** (or dual-SIM / eSIM devices) to arbitrage voice/data bundles and coverage across operators `[1, 6]`. Competition between Zain Iraq (4.5G+ LTE and digital youth brand *Oodi*) `[4]` and Asiacell (4G LTE and data-bundle tiers) `[5]` makes switching effortless: instead of formally cancelling a line, a dissatisfied user simply shifts outbound call and data volume to their secondary SIM until the primary SIM goes silent (`mou_Mean == 0`) `[1, 6]`. Regulatory actions by the CMC (such as numbering and license enforcement on Korek Telecom) and urban 4G quality gaps further accelerate subscriber switching `[3, 6]`.

3. **Q3 — Retention vs. acquisition economics & realistic churn benchmarks:**
   While the Company A calibration sample is balanced at `49.56%` churn to aid model training, empirical wireless telecom studies show that **realistic monthly churn is approximately `1.5%–2.5%` (`~1.8%/month` in the Duke/Teradata benchmark, or `5%–10%` over a multi-month campaign horizon)** `[7, 8]`. In saturated mobile markets (`>100%` SIM penetration), acquiring a replacement subscriber requires heavy dealer commissions, SIM/handset subsidies, and onboarding costs, whereas targeted retention preserves recurring monthly cash flow (`totmrc_Mean` / `rev_Mean`) at a fraction of acquisition cost `[7, 8]`. As Neslin et al. `[7]` and Verbeke et al. `[8]` demonstrate, even modest improvements in top-decile lift translate into hundreds of thousands of monetary units in net retained profit when campaigns target only the highest-expected-value segment.

4. **Q4 — Voluntary vs. involuntary churn and what an ML model can influence:**
   Telecom churn divides into **involuntary churn** (operator-initiated disconnection due to non-payment, credit default, or fraud) and **voluntary churn** (customer-initiated switching due to price/overage shock, aging handsets, or network quality) `[8, 9]`. An ML retention campaign can only profitably influence **persuadable voluntary churners**; offering discounts to involuntary credit defaulters or to "lost-cause" silent SIMs (`mou_Mean == 0`, who have already abandoned the line) wastes marketing budget `[8, 9]`. Our feature engineering and two-lane policy therefore separate credit/limit indicators (`crclscod`, `asl_flag`) from behavioral early-warning signals (`change_mou`, `overage_share`, `eqpdays`) and exclude silent SIMs from expensive incentive offers `[9]`.

5. **Q5 — Handset-upgrade and commitment cycles vs. churn:**
   In wireless markets, subscriber defection spikes sharply at the end of initial 12-month promotional commitments and when handsets pass roughly `10–12` months (`~300–360` days) of age without an upgrade `[7, 8]`. In our Company A training split, churn jumps by **`+23.83 pp` (`36.85%` at `260–300d` to `60.69%` at `301–340d`)** and peaks at **`64.57%` in month 11 of service**. However, because observational data cannot separate contract-expiry timing from pure hardware aging, Ascarza `[9]` shows that lifecycle interventions (such as a pre-300-day handset refresh or month-9 loyalty check) must be validated through a **randomized A/B holdout pilot** before full rollout.

6. **Q6 — Retention-campaign success rates and offer costs (Iraqi data gap $\rightarrow$ academic benchmarks + explicit `ASSUMPTION`s):**
   **No public Iraqi operator or CMC disclosure reports internal retention-campaign conversion rates (`s`) or per-customer offer costs (`c`)** `[3, 4, 5]`. In the international telecom churn literature (Neslin et al. `[7]`; Verbeke et al. `[8]`; Ascarza `[9]`), proactive retention-offer acceptance/save rates typically range from **`10%` to `30%`**, and per-contact incentive/call costs represent roughly **`10%–25%` of one month's ARPU**. Accordingly, following PRD §7 and §9.2, we label `s` (`10% / 20% / 30%`), `c` (`5 / 8 / 12 MU`), gross margin (`60% / 70% / 80%`), and retained horizon `H` (`3 / 6 / 9 months`) explicitly as **ASSUMPTION (sensitivity-tested across Conservative, Base, and Optimistic scenarios)** in `docs/NOTES.md → Assumptions`, while anchoring ARPU (`58.72 MU` mean, `48.20 MU` median) to the dataset and realistic churn priors ($\pi_r \in \{5\%, 10\%\}$) to `[7]` and `tutorial.ipynb`.

7. **Q7 — Peer-reviewed papers on telecom churn prediction & why tree models win on tabular data:**
   Four peer-reviewed studies anchor our methodology: (i) **Grinsztajn, Oyallon, & Varoquaux (NeurIPS 2022)** `[11]` prove across 45 tabular benchmarks that gradient-boosted trees (`XGBoost`, `LightGBM`) outperform deep neural networks on heterogeneous tabular data because axis-aligned splits capture sharp step discontinuities (like our `300`-day and `10–12`-month cliffs) and ignore uninformative/redundant columns natively; (ii) **Chen & Guestrin (KDD 2016)** `[12]` show that `XGBoost`'s sparsity-aware split finding learns optimal default directions for missing values (`NaN`), crucial for our `43` columns with informative missingness; (iii) **Verbeke et al. (EJOR 2012)** `[8]` prove across 11 telecom datasets that top-$k\%$ profit-maximizing targeting beats threshold accuracy; and (iv) **Saerens, Latinne, & Decaestecker (Neural Computation 2002)** `[10]` provide the exact Bayes label-shift formula to re-base predictions from our `49.56%` sample prior to realistic `5%–10%` operational churn priors.
